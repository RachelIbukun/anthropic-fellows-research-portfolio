# Literature Review

## Paper

How AI Impacts Skill Formation

## Summary

This paper investigates whether AI assistance improves learning outcomes or primarily increases short-term productivity.

The central question is whether AI systems help individuals build expertise or whether they reduce opportunities for deeper learning by performing a significant portion of the cognitive work on behalf of users.

The research explores how AI assistance affects performance, knowledge acquisition, and long-term skill development.

---

## Key Findings

The paper suggests that AI systems can improve immediate task performance and productivity.

However, increased productivity does not automatically translate into increased expertise.

In some circumstances, reliance on AI assistance may reduce opportunities for independent problem-solving and deeper understanding.

---

## Relevance to AI Security

These findings are highly relevant to AI-assisted software development.

If developers increasingly rely on AI-generated code and recommendations, organisations may experience:

* Increased productivity
* Reduced troubleshooting experience
* Reduced exposure to debugging processes
* Reduced opportunities to develop security judgement

Over time, this may affect an organisation's ability to detect vulnerabilities, investigate incidents, and evaluate AI-generated outputs.

---

## Relevance to Economics & Policy

The paper raises important questions relating to:

* Workforce development
* Future of work
* Human capital formation
* Productivity measurement
* Organisational resilience

Policy discussions around AI adoption should consider not only productivity gains but also long-term impacts on expertise development.

---

## Open Questions

* How does AI affect security expertise development?
* How does AI affect incident response capability?
* Which skills remain resilient to AI assistance?
* Which skills are most vulnerable to degradation?

---

## Personal Reflection

The most important insight from this paper is that productivity and expertise are not necessarily the same thing.

As AI becomes increasingly integrated into engineering workflows, organisations may need new ways to measure success that account for both output and capability development.
