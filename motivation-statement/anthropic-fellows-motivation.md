# Motivation Statement

My primary interest is the AI Security & Frontier Red Team workstream, with Economics & Policy as my secondary preference.

The reason I am applying to the Anthropic Fellows Programme is because I believe that ensuring advanced AI systems remain secure, reliable, and beneficial for society will be one of the defining challenges of this century. As AI systems become increasingly capable, they will not simply be tools that support human decision-making; they will become active participants in critical domains including software engineering, cybersecurity, scientific research, infrastructure operations, and economic systems. This creates extraordinary opportunities, but it also creates new categories of risk that require rigorous empirical research, careful evaluation, and thoughtful governance.

Throughout my career as a Cloud Engineer, I have worked on securing complex technical systems. My work has involved designing cloud infrastructure, implementing security controls, automating vulnerability management, securing software supply chains, and improving the resilience of platforms that support business-critical operations. One lesson has remained consistent across every environment I have worked in: capability without security creates risk. A system can be highly functional and still be unsafe if it is vulnerable to misuse, failure, or unintended consequences. I believe the same principle applies to advanced AI systems.

This is why AI security matters so deeply to me. As frontier models become capable of generating code, identifying vulnerabilities, conducting research, and interacting with increasingly complex environments, the consequences of security failures become significantly more important. The challenge is no longer limited to defending systems from external attackers; increasingly, we must understand how advanced models themselves can be manipulated, misused, or behave unexpectedly. Understanding these risks before they emerge at scale is, in my view, one of the most important research problems in AI today.

I am particularly drawn to Anthropic's Frontier Red Team work because it recognises that powerful systems cannot simply be trusted because they perform well on conventional benchmarks. Frontier models operate in ways that are fundamentally different from traditional software. They can exhibit behaviours that are difficult to predict, difficult to test exhaustively, and difficult to understand using conventional engineering approaches. This makes rigorous evaluation, adversarial testing, and empirical investigation essential. I am excited by research that seeks to uncover hidden failure modes, stress-test assumptions, and identify weaknesses before those weaknesses become real-world harms.

Equally important is the challenge of secure deployment. My professional experience has taught me that security cannot be treated as an afterthought. Systems must be designed with monitoring, governance, observability, and resilience in mind from the outset. The same principle applies to advanced AI. Even highly capable models can create significant risks if they are deployed without appropriate safeguards, evaluation frameworks, or mechanisms for oversight. I am particularly interested in understanding how organisations can responsibly deploy increasingly capable AI systems while maintaining security, accountability, and public trust.

My interest in Economics & Policy stems from the belief that AI safety is not solely a technical challenge. Advanced AI systems will influence labour markets, productivity, decision-making, education, cybersecurity, and public institutions. Understanding these impacts requires more than engineering expertise; it requires an understanding of incentives, governance structures, human behaviour, and societal outcomes. I am particularly interested in research exploring how AI affects professional expertise, skill development, and organisational decision-making, as well as how policymakers and institutions can build resilience against emerging AI-related risks.

My legal background has significantly shaped how I think about these questions. Legal training develops the ability to reason through uncertainty, evaluate evidence, analyse competing interests, and understand how rules influence behaviour. It has taught me to approach complex problems from both technical and human perspectives. Combined with my engineering experience, this has led me to view AI security, governance, and deployment as fundamentally socio-technical challenges that require collaboration across disciplines rather than purely technical solutions.

What attracts me most to Anthropic is its commitment to rigorous empirical research, intellectual honesty, and long-term thinking. The organisation consistently asks difficult questions about how advanced AI systems behave, how they should be evaluated, and how society can benefit from increasingly powerful models while mitigating potential harms. This approach strongly resonates with my own interests and values.

While I do not come from a traditional AI research background, I bring a combination of cloud engineering, cybersecurity, automation, software supply-chain security, risk analysis, and legal reasoning that I believe would contribute meaningfully to the Fellows Programme. I am eager to learn from Anthropic's researchers, contribute to ambitious projects, and develop the skills required to conduct impactful research at the intersection of AI security, policy, and society.

Ultimately, my goal is to help ensure that advanced AI systems are not only powerful, but also secure, trustworthy, and aligned with the interests of society. I see the Anthropic Fellows Programme as an exceptional opportunity to contribute to that mission while developing as a researcher and practitioner focused on some of the most important challenges of our time.
